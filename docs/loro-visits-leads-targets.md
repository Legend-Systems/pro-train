LORO Training Guide — Visits, Leads & Targets

Use this for a Google Meet session with your team. Plan about 45 to 60 minutes: live demo on screen, then questions at the end.


What this session is about

LORO tracks the work you do in the field — who you visited, who you called, and what new leads you brought in. Management sets targets for you (visits, calls, leads, and sometimes sales). This guide shows you how to log your work properly so it all counts.


The most important thing to remember

Starting your shift and starting a visit are two different things.

When you swipe to start your shift on the home screen, you are clocking in for the day. That tracks your attendance and working hours. It does not count as a visit or a call toward your targets.

When you tap the Visit button, you are logging contact with a customer or prospect. That is what counts toward your targets — but only after you end the visit.

So every time you speak to a customer — in person, on the phone, by email, or on WhatsApp — you need to start a visit and then end it when you are done. Just starting your shift is not enough.


How to log a visit on the phone app

Starting a visit

Open the app and go to Home. Tap Visit, then New visit.

The app will ask you to pick how you made contact:

- Physical — you went to see them in person. This counts as a visit.
- Telephone — you called them. This counts as a call.
- Email — you emailed them. This counts as a call.
- WhatsApp — you messaged them on WhatsApp. This counts as a call.

Allow location access when asked. The app records where you were when you started. You will see a message that your visit has started.

You can only have one visit open at a time. If you already have one running, the app will take you straight to ending it instead of starting a new one.

If you have no signal, you can still start a visit. It saves on your phone and uploads when you are back online.


Ending a visit — this is when it counts

When you are finished with the customer, tap Visit again. The app opens a form called Visit Details.

Fill in as much as you can:

- Link the visit to an existing client if you have one.
- Add the person's name, phone, and email if they are not already in the system.
- Add the company name and type of business.
- Write notes about what was discussed and what was agreed.
- Set a follow-up date if you need to go back or call again.
- Add a sale amount or quotation if you quoted or sold something.
- Add a photo if you visited in person.

When you submit, you will see a message that your visit has ended. That is the moment it counts toward your target. If you start a visit but never end it, it does not count.


Viewing your past visits

On the phone: tap Visit, then View history.

On the web: open Visits from the menu. You can see a list, filter by date or person, and view visits on a map.


How to log a visit on the web

Go to Visits in the sidebar. Click Start visit, pick the contact method, and let the browser capture your location. When you are done, click End visit and fill in the details. For in-person visits on the web, you may need to add a photo when you check out.


How to create and manage leads

A lead is a new prospect — someone who might become a customer.

Where to find leads

On the phone: Home, then Leads.

On the web: open Leads from the menu.


Creating a lead yourself

On the web, click Create lead. You need to pick a branch and fill in at least a name, email, or phone number. You can add more detail if you have it — company, source, priority, estimated value, follow-up date, and so on.

On the phone, go to Leads and tap create. Your branch is filled in automatically.

As soon as the lead is saved and you are the owner, it counts toward your new leads target.


Creating a lead from a visit

Sometimes you do not need to create a lead manually. When you end a visit and add contact details for someone who is not already a client, the system can create a lead for you automatically.

You can also open a completed visit in your history and tap Convert to lead, as long as that visit is not already linked to a lead.


Lead statuses — what they mean

Pending — brand new lead, not yet worked.

Approved — qualified and ready to move forward.

Review — needs a closer look.

Converted — you won. This is a successful sale or conversion.

Declined — not going ahead.

Cancelled — closed without a win.

Discarded — removed from the active list.

To mark a lead as won, open it and change the status to Converted. You can add a short reason if you want.

You do not need a manager to approve each status change. Managers see your overall progress in Reports. You update your own leads as you work them.


What counts toward your targets

Visits and calls

Only finished visits count. Starting one is not enough — you must end it.

- In-person (Physical) = 1 visit
- Phone call (Telephone) = 1 call
- Email = 1 call
- WhatsApp = 1 call

Leads

Creating a new lead counts immediately. It must be assigned to you as the owner. If someone else owns the lead, it counts on their target, not yours.


The daily calls and leads rule

If you have both a daily calls target and a daily leads target, you do not always need to hit both separately. You meet the daily requirement if any of these is true:

- You hit your full calls number for the day, or
- You hit your full leads number for the day, or
- Your calls and leads added together reach the higher of the two daily targets

Example: if both targets are 60 per day, you can do 30 calls and 30 leads and you are fine. Or 60 calls and no leads. Or 60 leads and no calls.

Visits are separate. You still need to hit your daily visit target on its own.


Performance warnings

When you end your shift for the day, the system checks whether you met your daily visits and your daily calls/leads requirement. If you did not, you may get a performance warning. These can escalate if it keeps happening (level 1, then 2, then 3). You will see it in the app. Your manager can see it in Reports under Targets.

If you start meeting your targets again, the warning clears. Very short shifts (under about 8 hours) may not trigger a warning.


How to check if you are on track

On the web, open Pipeline to see your progress for the current period — visits, calls, leads, and sales with progress bars.

The Dashboard also shows target progress at a glance.

Managers use Reports, Targets tab, to see the whole team.

On the phone, check your Profile for a summary of your targets.

The numbers show how you are doing against what was set for you — for example, 12 visits out of 20 means you are 60% of the way there for visits.


What to demo on Google Meet

Walk through these on screen share:

1. Show the home screen. Point out the Attendance button (shift) and the Visit button (customer contact). They are not the same.

2. Start a Physical visit, allow location, then end it with contact details and notes. Open Pipeline and show the visit count go up.

3. Start a Telephone visit, end it, and show the calls count go up — not visits.

4. Create a lead on the Leads page and show the leads count go up. Change its status to Converted.

5. End a visit with contact info but no client linked, and show the lead that was created — or use Convert to lead from visit history.

6. Open Pipeline and Reports so people know where to check their numbers.


Mistakes to avoid

Only starting your shift and never logging visits — your targets will stay at zero. Log every customer contact as a visit.

Starting a visit but forgetting to end it — it stays "in progress" and does not count. Always end the visit the same day.

Picking the wrong contact type — in-person must be Physical. Phone, email, and WhatsApp all count as calls, not visits.

Creating a lead under the wrong person — it counts on whoever owns it. Check you are the owner before saving.

Working offline and not syncing — connect to the internet and wait for the confirmation that your data uploaded.

Trying to start a second visit while one is still open — end the current one first.


Quick summary to paste in the Meet chat

Every customer contact:
1. Start your shift once at the beginning of the day.
2. Start a visit, pick the contact type, do your work, then end the visit with details.

What counts:
- Finished in-person visit = +1 visit
- Finished phone, email, or WhatsApp contact = +1 call
- New lead created = +1 lead

Where to check your progress:
- Web: Pipeline page
- Phone: Profile
- Managers: Reports, Targets tab


Questions to ask the team at the end

What is the difference between starting your shift and starting a visit?

When does a visit actually count toward your target?

Where do you go to see if you are on track for the month?

What happens if you forget to end a visit?
